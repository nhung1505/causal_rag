\begin{abstract}
Large language models and Retrieval-Augmented Generation remain limited on
legal questions requiring multi-hop condition--consequence reasoning. We
propose \textbf{LegalSCM-RAG}, which combines causal-path retrieval,
three-valued normative rule inference, and selective structural verification.
Legal events are represented by separate true and false support channels, while
sparse body/head incidence matrices encode conjunction within a rule and
alternative disjunctive support across rules. We further distinguish
node-deletion reachability, used only as a topological diagnostic, from the
structural intervention $do(X_M=0)$, which fixes mediator $M$ to false, replaces
its mechanism, and recomputes the rule fixed point.

On 250 graph-derived Vietnamese Penal Code questions, Full LegalSCM achieves
96.80\% decision accuracy, compared with 88.80\% for Causal Path RAG and
55.20\% for Vanilla RAG. The 8.0-point paired improvement over Causal Path RAG
is statistically significant (95\% CI: 4.4--12.0; Holm-adjusted
$p=0.0011$), whereas Answer F1, ROUGE-L, and Citation F1 do not differ
significantly. Closed-book controls reach 4.40\% with abstention and 9.20\%
under forced binary decisions, reflecting behavior under the verification
contract rather than general legal competence. Seven $do(X_M=0)$ interventions
are executed, but Causal Path RAG already answers all seven corresponding
questions correctly. The results therefore support the overall selective
verification mechanism, while the isolated benefit of structural intervention
remains to be established.

\end{abstract}
