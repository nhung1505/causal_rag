The low closed-book scores are not caused by failed runs or parsing errors.
With abstention enabled, the model returns \textsc{uncertain} on 220 questions;
because the gold set contains no \textsc{uncertain} label, only 11 predictions
are correct. Under forced binary decisions, it predicts
\textsc{reject-direct-claim} on 247 of 250 questions and achieves 9.20\%
accuracy. Thus, removing abstention produces a near-uniform rejection bias
rather than recovering legal competence. Since an always-\textsc{supported}
classifier would obtain 92\% raw accuracy on this imbalanced benchmark,
Balanced Accuracy and Macro-F1 are more informative than Accuracy alone.

\subsection{Effect of Selective Verification}

Full LegalSCM correctly answers 242 questions, compared with 222 for Causal
Path RAG. Full alone is correct on 23 samples, Causal Path RAG alone on 3,
both on 219, and neither on 5. The resulting 8.0-point improvement has a paired
bootstrap 95\% CI of $[4.4,12.0]$, with exact McNemar
$p=8.8\times10^{-5}$ and Holm-adjusted $p=0.0011$. Macro-F1 increases from
76.30\% to 91.67\%, while negative-class F1 increases from 59.02\% to 85.11\%.

The differences in free-form generation are substantially smaller: Answer F1,
ROUGE-L, and Citation F1 change by $+0.33$, $+0.52$, and $+0.47$ points,
respectively. Their confidence intervals contain zero, and all three
Holm-adjusted $p$-values equal 1.0. The measured improvement is therefore
specific to structured decision accuracy.

All 23 Full-only gains occur after verifier commitment: 21 come from factual
validation and 2 from direct-edge checking. None comes from the intervention
route. In the 20 fallback cases, Full produces no exclusive gain, while Causal
Path RAG alone is correct on 3 samples.

All eight Full errors occur after the no-primary-path fallback; seven are
reverse-reasoning questions. No error occurs after a committed verifier result.
Among the 20 negative samples, Full is correct on all 13 direct-edge cases and
all 7 mediator-intervention cases, compared with 90\% overall for Causal Path
RAG. However, Causal Path RAG also answers all 7 intervention cases correctly.
The executed interventions are therefore auditable but provide no isolated
paired advantage on this subset.

\subsection{Retrieval, Path Ranking, and Runtime}

Causal Path RAG and Full share identical retrieval outputs. Their Article,
Rule, and Event Recall@5 scores are 75.40\%, 71.20\%, and 74.79\%,
respectively, while Vanilla RAG obtains 60.07\% Article Recall@5. Top-1 Exact
Path is 38.56\%, compared with 75.42\% Oracle Exact Path; the gap indicates
that a relevant path is often retrieved but not ranked first.

Full requires 3.238 seconds per question, compared with 1.947 seconds for
Causal Path RAG. Selective verification therefore yields an 8.0-point
decision-accuracy improvement at approximately 66\% additional runtime.
