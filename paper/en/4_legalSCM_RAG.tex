\section{LegalSCM-RAG}
\label{sec:legalscm_rag}

LegalSCM-RAG integrates causal-path retrieval, query-aware LegalSCM
verification, and evidence-grounded answer generation. Given question $q$, the
pipeline is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
q
&\rightarrow
\text{Causal-Path Retrieval}
\rightarrow P^*\\
&\rightarrow
\text{Query-Aware Verification}
\rightarrow (z,\mathcal{E}^*)\\
&\rightarrow
\text{Answer Generation}
\rightarrow (a,C),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:overall_pipeline}
\end{equation}
where $P^*$ is the selected reasoning path, $z$ is the query-relative decision,
$\mathcal{E}^*$ is the final evidence, $a$ is the answer, and $C$ is the set of
statutory citations.

\subsection{Causal-Path Retrieval}

The system embeds the question and retrieves relevant events and rules from
causal memory. Retrieved events serve as seeds for expanding the directed
condition--consequence graph and constructing candidate paths
$\mathcal{P}(q)$. Each path is ranked using semantic and structural signals:
\begin{equation}
S(P_i,q)=
\alpha S_{\mathrm{sem}}
+\beta S_{\mathrm{graph}}
+\gamma S_{\mathrm{seed}}
+\delta S_{\mathrm{path}}.
\end{equation}
The primary path is selected as
\begin{equation}
P^*=\arg\max_{P_i\in\mathcal{P}(q)}S(P_i,q).
\end{equation}
Unlike conventional RAG, the retrieved context contains legal events, rules,
and the directed relations connecting them rather than only independent text
passages.

\subsection{Query-Aware LegalSCM Verification}

LegalSCM implements the sparse symbolic counterpart of the three-valued
body/head incidence formulation in Section~\ref{sec:problem_formulation}. It
iteratively applies the retrieved rules until reaching a fixed point over
$\{\mathrm{TRUE},\mathrm{FALSE},\mathrm{UNKNOWN}\}$.

The verifier selects one of three routes:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item a direct claim $A\rightarrow C$ is evaluated by checking whether a
    grounded direct edge exists between its endpoints;
    \item a standard legal question receives factual LegalSCM validation of
    the selected path without intervention; and
    \item an explicit mediator-removal or necessity question invokes
    $do(X_m=0)$ and compares the factual and intervened fixed points.
\end{enumerate}

For the third route, $do(X_m=0)$ fixes mediator $m$ to false, disables the rule
mechanisms that determine it, and recomputes the rule fixed point. This is
distinct from testing reachability in
$G^{-m}=G[V\setminus\{m\}]$. Node deletion is retained only as a bounded
topological diagnostic and never substitutes for the structural intervention
\cite{pearl2009causality}. The resulting necessity status is relative to the
encoded normative rule model and is not an estimate of a statistical causal
effect or unit-level actual causation.

\subsection{Selective Commit Gate}

A verifier result becomes authoritative only when the relevant grounding,
primary-path coverage, confidence, and state-consistency checks pass. We define
\begin{equation}
G_{\mathrm{commit}}
=
\mathbb{I}
[
g_{\mathrm{ground}}
\land g_{\mathrm{path}}
\land (s_{\mathrm{SCM}}\geq\tau)
\land g_{\mathrm{consistent}}
].
\end{equation}
The final decision and evidence are then selected by
\begin{equation}
z=
\begin{cases}
z_{\mathrm{SCM}}, & G_{\mathrm{commit}}=1,\\
z_{\mathrm{LLM}}, & G_{\mathrm{commit}}=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\mathcal{E}^*=
\begin{cases}
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{verified}}, & G_{\mathrm{commit}}=1,\\
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{path}}, & G_{\mathrm{commit}}=0.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:selective_commit}
\end{equation}

When the gate fails, the system does not convert a topological diagnostic into
an SCM decision and does not automatically force \textsc{uncertain}. Instead,
it preserves the original Causal Path RAG evidence and lets the common
generation model make the decision. Full LegalSCM is therefore a selective
hybrid rather than an SCM-authoritative classifier for every question.

\subsection{Answer Generation and Auditability}

The language model receives
\begin{equation}
I_q=(q,P^*,z,\mathcal{E}^*)
\end{equation}
and generates
\begin{equation}
(a,C)=f_{\theta}(I_q).
\end{equation}
All compared systems use the same generation schema and decoding settings.
LegalSCM-RAG additionally records the selected route, grounding results,
intervention status, commit decision, fallback status, and final evidence.
This separation allows retrieval, rule inference, structural intervention, and
answer generation to be audited independently.
