\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Large Language Models (LLMs) perform well on many question-answering tasks, but
reliable legal QA requires both relevant statutory evidence and a traceable
reasoning chain connecting conditions, intermediate events, and legal
consequences \cite{chalkidis2020legalbert,guha2023legalbench}.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) provides external evidence before
generation \cite{lewis2020retrieval,gao2024ragsurvey}; however, conventional
semantic retrieval often treats passages independently and does not explicitly
represent multi-hop condition--consequence relations.

We propose \textbf{LegalSCM-RAG}, which integrates a Legal Structural Causal
Model (LegalSCM) with RAG through causal-path retrieval and question-aware
structural verification. The graph represents normative dependencies extracted
from statutes rather than causal effects estimated from observational data. Its
rule layer follows three-valued fixed-point semantics
\cite{kleene1952metamathematics,fitting1985kripke} and uses a sparse
linear-algebraic representation motivated by vector- and tensor-space logic
programming \cite{sato2020threevalued,sakama2021tensor}. Body/head incidence
matrices encode conjunction among literals within a rule and alternative
disjunctive support across rules sharing the same consequent.

The verifier routes direct claims to grounded edge checking, ordinary questions
to factual rule inference, and explicit mediator-removal questions to the hard
intervention $do(X_m=0)$. We distinguish this intervention from node-deletion
reachability: testing reachability in $G^{-m}$ is only a bounded topological
diagnostic, whereas $do(X_m=0)$ fixes the mediator variable $X_m$ to false,
replaces its structural mechanism, and recomputes the rule fixed point. A
selective commit gate accepts a verifier decision only when grounding, path
coverage, confidence, and state-consistency conditions are satisfied.

The main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a framework for retrieving and ranking multi-hop paths over statutory
    condition--consequence relations;
    \item a three-valued linear-algebraic rule formulation supporting
    conjunction, alternative disjunctive mechanisms, and explicit structural
    intervention; and
    \item a controlled five-configuration evaluation with paired significance
    tests and route, commit, abstention, and intervention audits.
\end{itemize}

On 250 graph-derived Vietnamese Penal Code questions, Full LegalSCM achieves
96.80\% decision accuracy, compared with 88.80\% for Causal Path RAG and
55.20\% for Vanilla RAG. Its paired 8.0-point improvement over Causal Path RAG
is significant (95\% CI: 4.4--12.0; Holm-adjusted $p=0.0011$), whereas Answer
F1, ROUGE-L, and Citation F1 do not differ significantly. Closed-book accuracy
remains low both with abstention (4.40\%) and forced binary decisions (9.20\%),
indicating behavior under the verification-oriented contract without retrieved
legal evidence rather than general LLM competence. Although seven
$do(X_m=0)$ interventions are executed, Causal Path RAG also answers those
seven questions correctly. The results therefore support the overall selective
verification mechanism, while the isolated benefit of structural intervention
requires further evaluation.
