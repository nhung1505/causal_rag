\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

\subsection{Data and Compared Systems}

We evaluate on 250 multi-hop questions constructed from the Vietnamese Penal
Code \cite{blhs2025} and its derived legal graph. Among them, 236 have a linear
gold path. The decision labels comprise 230 \textsc{supported} and 20
\textsc{reject-direct-claim} samples, with no gold \textsc{uncertain}. The
router handles 13 negative samples by direct-edge checking and 7 explicit
mediator-removal samples using $do(X_m=0)$. Gold and evaluation fields are
never accessed during prediction generation. Because the questions and
retrieval graph share the same structured source, the experiment primarily
measures controlled internal validity.

We compare: (1) an abstaining closed-book LLM; (2) a forced-binary closed-book
LLM; (3) Vanilla RAG; (4) Causal Path RAG; and (5) Full LegalSCM. The four
abstention-enabled systems use the same generator, prompt rules, output schema,
context budget, and decoding parameters. The forced-binary variant changes
only the decision policy and is treated as a sensitivity control. No system
uses an extractive answer fallback.

\subsection{Metrics and Statistical Testing}

We report Decision Accuracy, Balanced Accuracy, and Macro-F1. Because no gold
sample is \textsc{uncertain}, the latter two are averaged over the two
gold-supported classes. Token-F1 and ROUGE-L evaluate answers, while set-based
F1 evaluates citations. Applicable retrieval components are evaluated using
Recall@5, MRR, and MAP; Top-1 and Oracle Exact Path evaluate selected and
candidate paths. Oracle scores are diagnostic only. Missing or failed
predictions receive zero on shared end-to-end metrics, and runtime is averaged
over successful predictions.

All comparisons are paired on the same 250 questions. We use 5,000 bootstrap
samples for 95\% confidence intervals, exact McNemar for Decision Accuracy, and
5,000 paired randomization sign flips for Answer F1, ROUGE-L, and Citation F1,
with Holm correction. We also audit the verification route, commit decision,
fallback, and whether $do(X_m=0)$ is requested and executed.
